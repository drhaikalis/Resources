DBT · Interpersonal Effectiveness

Asking for what you need, while remembering why you care

Most of us were never taught how to ask for something directly, say no, or stay close to someone while doing either.

a note before we begin

Two goals, and why they can pull against each other

Many relationship struggles come down to people being too aggressive (which can offend or escalate others, leading to short term harm) or too passive (which builds resentment and errodes relationships in the long term).

Effective assertiveness is our goal.

DBT teaches two important skills that can work together to help.

DEAR MAN gets into the specifics of how to make an ask or say no.

GIVE gets into how to interact in a way that will protect your relationships.

Sometimes, both will feel equally important. Sometimes, you'll prioritize one over the other. Don't make the choice on autopilot. You get decide your approach in each situation.

objectives effectiveness

DEAR MAN — getting what you want, asking clearly

DEAR MAN is designed as a sequence, not a menu of options.

Describing the situation comes before expressing feelings about it, because a feeling without a fact underneath it is easy to dismiss.

Expressing comes before asking/asserting, because people are far more willing to give something once they understand why it matters to the person asking.

Asserting comes next, saying specifically what you want in clear words, followed by holding that position while negotiating the edges.

It can be tempting to skip to asserting. Or to focus on blame/judgement instead of your own emotional experience. DEARMAN helps you stay focused on what is most important, in a way that is most likely to be heard.

Objective skill
D
Describe

State the facts of the situation. No mind-reading, no labels. Just what happened.

Tap for example ↻
What you might say

"You've been about twenty minutes late the last three times we've met up for dinner."

Objective skill
E
Express

Say your feeling(s) out loud. Don't assume it's obvious. You can also share opinions here, but feelings are the priority.

Tap for example ↻
What you might say

"I feel a little brushed off when that happens, and it makes me feel like you aren't thinking about the impact on me."

*You might share more vulnerable feelings (lonely, abandoned, hurt) with someone you're closer with, like family or a close friend, and less vulnerable feelings (confused, disappointed, frustrated) with an acquaintance or coworker.

Objective skill
A
Assert

Ask for what you want, or say no, without hedging it into invisibility. Be specific.

Tap for example ↻
What you might say

"I want you to stop putting me in that situation. Would you be willing to text with updates while you are getting ready so that I know when you are actually on the way?"

Objective skill
R
Reinforce

Tell them what's in it for them. Oftentimes, you are making the ask to protect the relationship long term. To stay close.

Tap for example ↻
What you might say

"That way, I can stay back at my place if you are running late and be able to use that time for other things I want to do. It will also make me feel like you are considering my feelings."

Objective skill
M
(stay in the) Moment

If their response doesn't address your ask, don't chase the tangent. Repeat your ask like a broken record if you have to.

Tap for example ↻
What you might say

"I know that last week was especially hectic for you, and I still think texting me while you are getting ready with updates on your ETA will make a difference going forward."

Objective skill
A
Appear confident

Steady voice, steady eyes. Confidence is a posture you can adopt before you feel it.

Tap for example ↻
What I might notice myself doing

Keeping my voice level and my gaze on them, even while my stomach is doing something else entirely.

Objective skill
N
Negotiate

Be prepared to offer some flexibility, once you take into account their perspective. Give to get. If stuck, try handing the problem back: "what do you think would work?"

Tap for example ↻
What you might say

"If you don't think you can do that, maybe we should just hang at each other's houses for a while, or pick each other up before dinner. What do you think would be more doable?"

relationship effectiveness

GIVE — keeping the relationship intact

GIVE runs at the same time as DEAR MAN, not after it. It's the tone underneath the words. The exact same request, made with GIVE or without it, can produce a completely different relationship on the other side — even when the answer is identical.

This is the piece that tends to get skipped in anger or overdone out of fear of conflict. Used well, it doesn't soften the ask, it just keeps the door open after the ask has been made.

Relationship skill
G
(be) Gentle

No attacks, no threats, no eye-rolling. Say the hard thing calmly and stay in the room.

Tap for example ↻
What I might say

"I felt disappointed you didn't call on my birthday" — not "you never think about anyone but yourself."

Relationship skill
I
(act) Interested

Listen like you mean it. Face them, don't interrupt, let them finish before you respond.

Tap for example ↻
What that looks like

Putting the phone down, facing them, and asking "what happened next?" instead of jumping to my own story.

Relationship skill
V
Validate

Say what you see from their side, out loud. Validating isn't agreeing, it's acknowledging.

Tap for example ↻
What I might say

"I can see why that would be frustrating for you" — even if I still need to ask for something different.

Relationship skill
E
(use an) Easy manner

A little lightness. Soft sell over hard sell. Leave the attitude at the door.

Tap for example ↻
What I might say

"I'd love to, but I've already got plans that night. Let's find another time though!"

Ugh...Just drafting the "DEAR" is so hard...

If you notice yourself taking a long time drafting what you want to say or overthinking what you've written, that rumination is costing you resources you'll need later, for the response.

Instead, try writing a rough draft quickly, then handing it to an LLM like Claude or ChatGPT. Ask it to evaluate the draft from the perspective of a DBT therapist with expertise in assertive, effective communication. Make any small edits to ensure it is in your own voice and send. It's a fast way to sharpen the ask without spending your whole evening on it.

A caveat

DEAR MAN and GIVE are not a guarantee — they're a way of stacking the odds in your favor. Sometimes the relationship has to take priority and you'll ask for less than you want. Sometimes the objective has to take priority and the relationship will feel some friction. That's a judgment call you make on purpose, not a failure of the skill.

And "broken record" is not the same as escalating. If the other person attacks or threatens, the instruction is to ignore the bait and keep restating your point — calmly, not louder.
