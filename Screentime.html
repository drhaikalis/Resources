Screen Time, Made Simple

You're one of the most important people in this child's life. Here's a rundown what the research says and what experts recommend. Use it to help you decide how to use screens around the kids in your life.

Their screen use

Why It Matters

Six things the research keeps finding. Tap any card to learn about that topic.

Language

In real life

1 hr of screens a day at age 1 → 60% higher risk of speech delay. At 4+ hrs, toddlers are 5× more likely to be delayed. Talking and singing builds language faster than any show.

Weight
tap ↻
In real life

Each extra hour per day (above the average) → 66% higher risk of unhealthy weight by age 6. Trading minutes of screens for active play adds up fast over a childhood.

Sleep
tap ↻
In real life

Screens in bed → nearly 4× more sleep problems: trouble falling asleep, night waking, and restlessness.

Eyesight
tap ↻
In real life

Each hour beyond 1/day → 21% higher risk of nearsightedness. Good news: outdoor light helps protect vision!

Emotions
tap ↻
In real life

Using a device to calm a child → more outbursts over time. Naming their feelings, being a calm presence, and sticking to limits will help them in the long-term.

Attention
tap ↻
In real life

Even TV "just in the background" is linked to weaker attention, language, and self-regulation. If it's not being watched together with intention, it's better for young brains to have it off.

Our Screens Matter Too

Kids notice when we're on our phones, too. Tap to flip.

You're not alone
Almost every parent does this — yes, really.
tap ↻
The numbers

89% of parents use a phone or device around their kids on a typical day; 70%+ during play or meals. This isn't a personal failing. It's the default in our tech-driven world if we don't target it directly.

The catch
Our phones affect them.
tap ↻
The research

Linked to weaker child attention, more acting out, and less secure bonding. It's also the top predictor of kids' own screen time.

Buzz buzz
Even the notifications you ignore matter — if you can hear them.
tap ↻
The research

In one study, the sound of phone notifications alone — no scrolling, no picking it up — predicted lower infant vocabulary. A buzzing phone counts as much as a scrolling one.

Easy wins, not perfection
Focus on limiting your use during the times that matter most.
tap ↻
Try this

Everyone's situation is different, and most of us find limiting phone use hard. Instead of an all-or-nothing goal that just fuels guilt, try "movie theater rules" during mealtime, playtime, and bedtime.

The experts say

Recommended Limits by Age

Under 18 mo

No screens, except video calls with family.

18 mo – 2 yrs

Most days none. Occasionally 10-30 min of a quality show, together.

2 – 5 yrs

Up to 30 min/day of quality shows, together. Occasionally up to 1 hr.

6 yrs & up

Consistent daily limit (~1–2 hrs). Sleep & activity come first.

Across every age, three windows matter most: feeds/bottles/meals, floor play, and bedtime. Reducing screens in these moments gives the biggest payoff. It's about protecting our richest opportunities for connection and learning.
Every age, every day

Four Suggested House Rules

No screens at meals, bedtime, or bedNot during meals, not while getting ready for bed, and never in the bedroom.

No "just background" TVEasy to lose track of, and not the together-time we're going for.

Skip the "calm-down" tabletTempting in the moment, but it makes self-soothing harder over time.

Phones away for us, tooAs often as you can during feeds/meals, floor play, and bedtime, adult phones go on silent and out of sight. Modeling matters.

The good stuff

Screen-Free Favorites

These matter more than you'd think.

Talk & read together

Narrate what you're doing. "Now I'm stirring the soup!"

Go outside

1–2 hrs/day for eyesight, mood, and energy levels (theirs and yours).

Cook, bake, or do chores

Measuring and pouring build math, motor skills, and pride.

Blocks, crayons, puzzles

Open-ended play builds creativity and problem-solving.

Tell stories

Storytime builds listening skills, and kids love it.

Cards or board games

Great for patience, turn-taking, and a little friendly competition.
