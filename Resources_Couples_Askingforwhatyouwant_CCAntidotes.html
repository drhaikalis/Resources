How to Ask for What You Want

Antidotes for Criticism and Contempt

Hurt or frustration is often tied to something we want to ask of our partner. When we sit on it, it builds into anger or resentment. Unstated wants and needs often come out as criticism or even contempt, two powerful forces that erode closeness.

I

Criticism

Criticism is a negative, generalized evaluation or character attack on your partner, stated directly or passive-aggressively, instead of a focused complaint or request about a specific behavior.

The Antidote
Ask for what you want with a “soft start-up.”
How to do it
Build your opening around 3 things:
I feel [emotion]... ...about [neutral description of the situation] ...and I need/want [positive request]
  1. "I feel [emotion]..." Lead with your own internal state, not an attack on your partner's character or a pile-up of similar past situations. Letting your partner into your inner world opens the door to understanding.
  2. "...about [neutral description of the situation]" Stick to concrete facts about the specific event, without "always" or "never." Emotion tends to give things a negative lens; staying objective serves you both.
  3. "...and I need/want [positive request]" Say clearly what you DO want moving forward. This turns a complaint or demand into an invitation for your partner to support you.

The soft start-up in action

Tap a card (or press Enter or Space) to turn it over. Tap again to turn it back.

Example 1

Seeing a mess in the kitchen

What it can sound like Criticism “You never help around the house. You are so lazy.”
What it can look like Bottling You clean the kitchen, tallying how many times it has been you and getting more annoyed. You zero in on this one task and stop noticing what your partner does that you like, like cooking breakfast every day this week.
What to say instead Soft start-up “I’m feeling let down that there is still a mess in the kitchen after you told me you’d handle it last night. Are you willing to take lead on cleaning up in here today?”
Example 2

Feeling disconnected from your partner

You’ve felt disconnected from your partner and tried planning a few dates, but they were too tired. Now your favorite comedian is coming to town and you’re hoping they’ll buy tickets. The closer it gets, the more you expect to be disappointed.

What it can sound like Criticism “You never put thought or effort into our relationship anymore. I’ve told you I’m the biggest fan of Taylor Tomlinson and that she is coming to town. Do you ever even listen to me?”
What it can look like Bottling You say nothing. When the day comes you’re a little edgy and make an excuse to go to bed early. You can’t enjoy spending time with your partner.
What to say instead Soft start-up “I’ve been feeling disconnected from you and am missing our date nights. I really want to go see Taylor Tomlinson and it would mean so much if you’d get tickets and plan the night for us! I can work on finding a sitter. What do you think?”

Remember: This is just the opening. Your partner may have a reason they can’t meet your request right now, but opening this way makes it more likely you’ll hear each other and reach a resolution.

II

Contempt

Contempt is communicating from superiority, or with disrespect, mockery, or sarcasm. It is the strongest predictor of relationship breakdown. Less contempt makes it easier to ask for what you want, and for each of you to want to give it.

The Antidote
Build a culture of appreciation, fondness, and admiration.
How to do it
Regularly express gratitude, respect, and affection, in daily routines and during difficult moments. This positive sentiment makes contempt hard to feel or express.
The Magic Ratio
Work towards 5:1 positive to negative interactions during conflict and 20:1 in everyday life. Even when upset, remind yourself and your partner of what you appreciate, validate their experience, laugh together, or offer affection if they’re open to it. If this feels far off, that’s okay; any increase helps.
5:1 during conflict
20:1 in everyday life
positivenegative

My main reminder comes from Kurt Vonnegut. If it resonates, write it down and put it where you’ll see it (e.g., fridge, pantry).

“… I urge you to please notice when you are happy, and exclaim or murmur or think at some point, ‘If this isn’t nice, I don’t know what is.’”

Ways to build appreciation into daily life

  • “Thank you for picking up the groceries today; it really lightened my load after a long meeting.”“Thanks for putting the dishes away.”“Thanks for taking the kids to their appointment today.”
  • “I love how loyal you are to your friends.”“I really admire your patience when things go sideways.”
  • “That was so funny.”“I love laughing with you.”
  • “What’s been on your mind today?”“How are you feeling about work lately?”“Tell me more about that.”
  • “I’m so happy you’re home.”“Come here, I missed you.”
  • “I love your smile.”“Those jeans look great on you.”“Your eyes are beautiful.”
  • “That’s awesome, tell me more!”“Congrats on the new high score!”“Great round of golf!”
  • “Look at how brave our kiddo was at soccer today. It was so fun watching with you.”“I love walking into our new bathroom. Your paint color choice is so relaxing.”
